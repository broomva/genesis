Genesis · design spec

Voice into an armed thread

Point the voice leg at a session you already have open, instead of a fresh one nobody remembers — and take the consent from the authenticated side, because a phone number is not a credential.

This is not the Option C spec. That one (realtime-voice-workspace-agent, PASS 8/10) builds a realtime conversational front with narration, barge-in and a dispatch capability. This is a much smaller thing that captures most of the same value: change one argument in the existing voice path, and add a gate deciding who may set it. No WebRTC, no contextual updates, no protocol spike.

What voice does today, measured

A call reaches an ElevenLabs agent, which records the request as a ticket. A poller in chat-bot drains the queue every 15s, runs the request as a real Genesis turn in the caller's tenant workspace, and sends the answer to their WhatsApp. It works — five answers delivered in the 24 hours to 2026-08-25 02:00.

Two properties limit it, and both are structural rather than incidental:

PropertyConsequence
threadId: voice:${ticket.id} — a fresh session per callNo memory. Two calls share nothing, and voice cannot see the conversation you are already having. Deliberate: the comment says sharing a thread "would bleed context between unrelated calls"
The answer is pushed to WhatsAppA phone call does not open Meta's 24-hour service window. If you have not messaged the bot recently the answer cannot be delivered — and that is only discoverable when the send fails

Worth stating plainly: every voice answer delivered so far has been a test prompt of 12–29 characters ("reply with exactly…"). No substantive voice answer has been observed end to end. The channel is proven to carry bytes; it is not proven to carry a useful reply.

The observation this rests on

threadId is the only thing that identifies a session. Verified, not assumed:

apps/api/src/server.ts:434     const threadId = body.threadId;
                               if (!threadId) return c.json({ error: "threadId required" }, 400);

packages/core/src/supervisor.ts:892
                               resumeSessionId: session.agentSessionId   ← same threadId, same --resume

And the voice drain already passes an arbitrary threadId — it just makes one up:

apps/chat-bot/src/index.ts
    genesisStream({ baseUrl, threadId: `voice:${ticket.id}`, text: ticket.request, workspaceId })

So dispatching a call into an existing session is a change to one argument. Everything expensive — the session, the worktree, --resume, the workspace binding — already exists and already works.

What that buys

The 24-hour window leaves the critical path

Nothing needs pushing to a phone. A turn on your thread is in your thread; you open the PWA and it is there. Today's worst failure — the answer exists and cannot be delivered — stops existing rather than being mitigated.

Voice gains memory

Same threadId → same agentSessionId → --resume. Voice becomes another way to talk to the same agent, mid-conversation, rather than a stranger who has to be re-briefed every call.

And what it costs — the blast radius inverts

The PWA session is genuinely trusted: a Better Auth passkey on your device. The voice leg is not. It arrives as a phone call identified by caller ID, and caller ID is spoofable. Trust in the workspace does not launder the identity of whoever dialled.

TodayDispatching into your PWA thread
A spoofed caller obtainsan answer in their own tenant workspacea turn executed inside your session and your worktree
Worst casethey waste your computethey act in your work

Compounding it, and verified rather than assumed: authorizePrincipal authenticates a principal — a Better Auth session or the machine token — and does not check thread ownership. No route does. That is safe today only because the deployment is single-owner. It becomes load-bearing the moment a call can name a thread.

This is the same lesson the Option C spec took five review rounds to reach, arriving from a different direction: consent must come from the authenticated side, never from the voice side. There it was "fresh speech is presence, not consent". Here it is "a phone number is not a credential".

The design: arm from the PWA, never from the call

The authenticated side nominates the target. A call is resolved against the armed set; it never supplies a thread id. So the worst a spoofed caller achieves is reaching a thread the real owner deliberately opened, during a window they deliberately opened.

StepWhoWhat
1You, in the PWAArm a thread for voice. One control, an explicit duration.
2BFFRecords (threadId, ownerId, callerId, expiresAt). Authorizes ownerId against the thread — the check that does not exist yet. The owner also names which caller may reach it.
3CallerSpeaks a request. The ticket carries callerId as it does now, and no thread.
4DrainResolves the caller to an armed thread. No match ⇒ exactly today's behaviour, tenant workspace and WhatsApp delivery.
5GenesisRuns the turn on that threadId, resuming the session.
6YouOpen the thread. The answer is in it. Nothing was pushed anywhere.

Two identities, and they are not the same thing

Review of this draft caught a real ambiguity: it used the word principal for both sides, and they live in different identity spaces. Conflating them is how an implementation ends up authorizing one identity and dispatching on the other.

ownerIdcallerId
Comes fromBetter Auth session — a passkey on your deviceparseVoicePrincipals, a normalized phone number from an env allowlist
Trustauthenticatedasserted, spoofable
Used forproving the arming request may touch this threadmatching an incoming call to an armed record

The mapping between them is asserted at arming time, by the authenticated side. The owner says "this thread, for this caller, for this long" — so the binding is a claim by the trusted party, never an inference from the untrusted one. A caller ID appearing in the allowlist proves only that the number is known; it never selects a thread on its own.

Arming expires, and that is the point. A standing permission is indistinguishable from no permission after a week. A window you opened for this call is a window you remember opening.

What must be built

1
Thread ownership authorization. The prerequisite, and the only part that is load-bearing for safety. authorizePrincipal authenticates; nothing checks that the principal owns the thread it is acting on. Single-owner today makes this invisible — arming makes it real. This is the same phase-1 item the Option C spec named, arrived at independently.
2
The armed set. POST /api/threads/:id/arm-voice taking a duration and a callerId; a store of (threadId, ownerId, callerId, expiresAt); expiry enforced on read, not by a sweeper. At most one armed record per callerId — arming a second supersedes the first, so resolution is a lookup and never a choice. Small enough to be a file beside the voice queue, where the rest of this channel's state already lives.
3
Resolution in the drain. callerId → the single armed record bearing it, if any → its threadId, passed to genesisStream in place of the synthetic one. Unarmed is not an error — it is the current path, untouched. Ambiguity cannot arise, because phase 2 permits only one armed record per caller; if one somehow does, refuse rather than pick.
4
The arming control in the PWA. Per-thread, showing the remaining window and offering to cancel. The visible state is the security property: if you cannot see what is armed, you cannot notice what should not be.
5
Dogfood. Arm a thread, call, and confirm the turn appears in that thread with continuity — that the agent knows what you were already discussing. Then let the arming expire and confirm the next call falls back cleanly to today's behaviour rather than erroring.

What this deliberately does not do

No realtime anything

No WebRTC, no contextual updates, no narration, no barge-in, no dispatch capability held by a voice model. That is the Option C spec and it is a much larger build with a protocol spike in front of it. Nothing here depends on it, and it does not block it.

No change to the unarmed path

A call with no armed thread behaves exactly as it does today — tenant workspace, WhatsApp delivery, followUp: "none". This adds a path; it does not replace one. Regression surface is a resolution step that can return nothing.

No confirmation of the transcript

Option C added a confirm-after-transcription step because a gesture proves intent but not wording. There is no screen in a phone call to confirm against. Named as an open question rather than waved at.

No multi-user story

Phase 1 makes ownership checkable, which is the precondition. Actually serving several users is a different piece of work and this does not pretend to be it.

Open questions — measure before building

  • Does the supervisor serialize concurrent turns on one threadId? Stated in the risks table as "already serializes" — I have not verified it. If it does not, two calls into one armed thread interleave in a worktree. Check before phase 3.
  • Is a mistranscribed destructive request an acceptable risk here? Today it is bounded by the tenant workspace. Armed, it is not. A read-only default with an explicit "you may change things" arming mode is the obvious answer and doubles the surface; worth deciding rather than defaulting.
  • Is the answer discoverable? The 24-hour window disappears because nothing is pushed — but nothing is pushed. If you do not open the PWA you do not know the answer arrived. A single short WhatsApp "answered in thread" would restore the notification without carrying the content, and would itself be subject to the 24h window.
  • Does this actually want the ElevenLabs agent at all? If the target is a thread you already have open, a push-to-talk in the PWA that posts a transcript to /api/chat is strictly simpler and needs no phone, no funnel, and no allowlist. The phone leg earns its place only when you are away from the device — which is the real use case, but worth saying out loud.
